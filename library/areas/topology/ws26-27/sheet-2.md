### Exercise 5: $R^n\cong R^m$ implies $n=m$

**Exercise 5.** Let $R$ be a commutative ring. Prove the following two statements.

- (a) If $\mathbb{Z}^n\cong\mathbb{Z}^m$, then $n=m$.
- (b) If $R^n\cong R^m$, then $n=m$. (*Hint:* Pick a maximal ideal …)

*Remark:* If $R$ is not assumed to be commutative, then (b) does not hold in general anymore.

### Exercise 9: which spaces are geometric realisations?

**Exercise 9.** This exercise addresses the question «Can every topological space be obtained as the geometric realisation of a simplicial complex?»

- (a) Show that the geometric realisation of a simplicial complex is always a Hausdorff space. (Hence the answer to the above question is «Obviously no.»)
- (b) Recall what it means for a topological space to be connected respectively path-connected. Can the geometric realisation of a simplicial complex be connected, but not path-connected?
- (c) Give an example of a Hausdorff space which cannot be attained as the geometric realisation of a simplicial complex.

### Exercise 10: the Baer–Specker group $\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}$ is not free

**Exercise 10.** Show that $\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}$ is not a free abelian group.

*Hint:* Proceed as follows.

1. Let $A$ be an abelian group. An element $a\in A$ is called *divisible* if for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$ there is a $b\in A$ such that $nb=a$. What are the divisible elements of a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module?
2. View $\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}\subset\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}$ as a subgroup. How many divisible elements does $\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}/\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}$ have (countably many or uncountably many)? Try to lead that to a contradiction under the assumption that the product were free.

Further reading: [The Baer–Specker group](https://wildtopology.com/2014/07/02/the-baer-specker-group/) on the Wild Topology blog.

*Comments to follow.*
